I am the Presidential Penn Compact Professor of Political Science at the University of Pennsylvania, where I co-direct PDRI-DevLab@Penn. My research sits at the intersection of development, redistribution, big data, and political communications. I work closely with governments, international organizations, and implementing agencies to design and evaluate development and governance programs.

My scholarship has appeared in leading academic journals and with Cambridge University Press. Current work spans several field projects and a number of big data initiatives. Ongoing field-based work focuses on: social perceptions of refugees and their impact on the prospects for integration in Kenya, the human impact of U.S. deportations of Central Americans, and the micro-decisionmaking of migration in Mexico and Ghana. On the big data side, several projects run through Machine Learning for Peace (MLP), which uses state-of-the-art natural language processing to monitor civic and political dynamics around the world. MLP processes more than 130 million news articles from 600+ outlets in nearly 40 languages and across 70+ countries to generate high-frequency data on civic life, Russian and Chinese foreign influence activities (Resurgent Authoritarian Influence, or RAI), and MLEED (Machine Learning for Environmental Event Detection) to measure individual, community, private sector, and governmental responses to climate change.

Mentorship & training

Through DevLab@Penn and PDRI, I mentor students and postdoctoral fellows at every career stage — from undergraduates to predocs and postdocs — through applied research, internships, and coursework, with projects in Central America, Ghana, Kenya, and Central Asia.
