PSCI 1102 — Global Development: Data, Politics and Practice

Undergraduate lecture · Fall · required for the International Development minor

Why are some countries — and some households — rich, and others poor? This course introduces the analytical tools for understanding why development varies across the world and the practical tools for designing and evaluating policies aimed at alleviating poverty. The first half of the course focuses on household-level dynamics of poverty and development — food, credit, health, education, migration, and day-to-day governance — and how field experiments are used to evaluate interventions. The second half turns to the macro picture: the political and economic foundations of sustained growth, including historical legacies, technology, and institutions. Students choose a country to research throughout the semester, working hands-on with household survey data.

PSCI 6110 — Comparative Political Analysis II

Graduate seminar · Spring · second course in the comparative politics field sequence

This PhD seminar is the second half of a two-course sequence preparing students for the comprehensive exam in comparative politics, and stands on its own for students interested in the methodological and theoretical debates surrounding the study of states and markets. Each week surveys a substantive area of the field — state formation and political order, ethnicity and identity, state capacity, growth and development, welfare regimes, corruption and rent-seeking, civil war and displacement, clientelism, regime transitions, authoritarian politics and backsliding, and the international political economy of migration and trade — often led by a guest faculty member whose research matches that week's theme. Students write two article reviews, lead one discussion with a replication exercise, and develop an original research proposal.
