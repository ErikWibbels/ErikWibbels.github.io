Schedule

Weekly topics, readings, and assignments. Full readings (and links) are also posted on Canvas.

Part I — The Micro View: Economics and Politics of Development

Week 1

Aug 26

Course intro. What are we doing here? Expectations and aspirations. What is “development”? What’s the relationship between household poverty and the global distribution of poverty?

Readings

  • Paul Collier, The Bottom Billion, Ch. 1 (available online through Penn Library)
  • Shane DeRolf, “Committing to a Randomized Evaluation: the scariest and smartest thing we ever did” (blog)
  • Huckstep et al. 2025, “Charting the Fallout of Aid Cuts: Which Countries Will be Hit Hardest, as Multiple Donors Cut Budgets?” (CGD blog)
  • Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie, “The Danger of a Single Story” (TED talk)

Preparation for next week

Find a country you are interested in and are going to work on this semester. Make sure it has micro- (household/survey) data available.

Week 1 Assignment

Week 2

Aug 31 & Sep 2

Conceptualizing “poverty” and “development.” The dimensions of poverty and wealth. The distribution of poverty and wealth across countries and households. The relationship between growth, inequality, and development.

Readings

  • Amartya Sen, Development as Freedom, Ch. 4, “Poverty as Capability Deprivation” (available on Canvas)
  • Ferreira and Sanchez-Paramo, “A Rich Array of International Poverty Lines” (blog)
  • Lonborg et al. 2025, “June 2025 global poverty update from the World Bank: 2021 PPPs and new country-data”
  • Borker and Legovini 2024, “Empowering women: Impact evaluation insights into labor force participation and economic inclusion” (Development Impact blog)

Discussion

Develop, justify, and compute a poverty measure from your country using survey data.

Week 2 Assignment

Week 3

Sep 9 (no class Sep 7 — Labor Day)

The proximate causes of economic growth and the role of politics and policies: labor, capital, technology, human capital, and the role of policy in development.

Readings

  • David N. Weil, Economic Growth, Chs. 2–3 (available on Canvas)
  • Naidu, Rodrik and Zukman, “Economics After Neoliberalism”; William Easterly, “In Defense of Neoliberalism” (Boston Review, Feb 2019)
  • JPAL, “Introduction to Randomized Evaluations”
  • Blattman et al., “Comparing the Impacts of Industrial Jobs and Self-Employment in Ethiopia” (blog)
  • Shotaro Nakamura 2022, “What happens when we directly regulate informal labor markets in developing economies” (blog)

Discussion

The ingredients of growth in your country — labor, capital, technology, human capital. Examine the cross-country relationship between growth and these indicators.

>Week 3 Assignment

Week 4

Sep 14 & 16

Social mobility and intergenerational transmission of income/wealth. How parents shape children. The ingredients of upward and downward social mobility.

Readings

  • Anirudh Krishna, One Illness Away, Chs. 1–2
  • Doepke et al. 2022, “Unequal Education and the ‘Great Gatsby Curve’” (CEPR blog)
  • JPAL, “Evaluating the Impact of Moving to Opportunity in the U.S.” (blog)

Discussion

Measuring social mobility through narratives, surveys, and censuses; gather an oral history of intergenerational mobility from your own or a friend’s family.

Week 4 Assignment

Week 5

Sep 21 & 23

Immediate needs — food and credit. How the quest for calories shapes the human condition. Planning for the future: how access (or not) to credit shapes household behavior.

Readings

  • Banerjee and Duflo, Poor Economics, Chs. 1, 6, 7 (available online through Penn Library)
  • N’Simire and Tchumisi, “The Benefits and Challenges of Financial Diaries as a Research Method in Goma, DRC” (blog)
  • Bedasso and Mendez Acosta 2025, “What Does It Cost to Feed a Child? New Global Evidence on the Economics of School Meals” (CGD blog)
  • JPAL, “Microcredit” (blog); JPAL 2021, “Designing financial services and social protection programs to enhance women’s economic empowerment”

Discussion (choose one)

Calorie counts and food costs in your country, or borrowing costs and returns to education in your country.

Week 5 Assignment

Week 6

Sep 28 & 30

Transfers and social policy — education and cash transfers. The importance (and difficulty) of incentivizing good performance by teachers and nurses. The boom in conditional and unconditional cash transfers.

Readings

  • Rossi, Lipsey, and Henry 2019, “Impact Evaluation” (Ch. 6 in Evaluation: A Systematic Approach)
  • Della Guardia et al. 2022, “Selective inclusion in cash transfer programs” (World Development, intro; available via Penn Library online)
  • Crosta et al. 2024, meta-analysis of unconditional cash transfer programs (intro)
  • Dylan Matthews, “Don’t teach a man to fish. Give him the goddamn fish” (blog)
  • Planet Money 2016, “The Risk Farmers”

Discussion

The mean and distribution of educational achievement outcomes in your place.

Week 6 Assignment

Week 7

Oct 5 & 7

Migration and urbanization. Migratory flows to urban areas and the growth of slums. Inter- vs. intra-national migration.

Readings

  • Vullnetari 2020, “The interface between internal and international migration” (in Routledge Handbook of Migration and Development; available via Penn Library online)
  • Mike Davis, Planet of Slums, Chs. 2–3 (available via Penn Library online)
  • Gibson, McKenzie, Rohorua, and Stillman 2018, “International migration’s long-term impact: Evidence from a lottery” (blog)
  • Huang, Leon and Berretta 2023, “What works for addressing root cause and drivers of irregular migration? There are few answers to pressing policy questions”

Discussion

The relationship between household well-being and migration in your country; using Google Earth to examine the evolution of your country’s capital city.

Week 7 Assignment

Week 8

Oct 12 & 14

Midterm on October 12 — no assignment this week. Governance: accessing bureaucrats, policing, corruption, and accountability. Clientelistic vs. programmatic politics. Citizen tech, engagement, and accountability.

Readings

  • Paul Collier, The Bottom Billion, Ch. 5 (available online through Penn Library)
  • Chris Blattman 2022, “The terrible trade-off: Why less violent cities often means more powerful and organized crime” (blog)
  • JPAL, blog post on Policing in Rajasthan, India
  • Haim and Ravanilla 2026, “EGAP Brief 87: How Does Community Policing Affect Police Attitudes? An Experimental Test and a Theory of Bureaucrat-Citizen Contact”

Week 9

Oct 19 & 21

Designing policies for alleviating poverty and improving governance. Designing randomized evaluations. How to evaluate whether a program or policy works.

Readings

  • Glennerster and Takavarasha 2013, Running Randomized Evaluations, Chs. 1–2 (available online via Penn Library)
  • Chris Blattman, “Why ‘what works?’ is the wrong question: Evaluating ideas not programs” (blog)
  • Oganisian and Mitra 2020, “Why It’s Hard to Evaluate State Policies in the Pandemic” (blog)

Discussion

Use Eve Vivalt’s Build Your Own Meta-Analysis tool to explore the range of evaluation outcomes on a policy outcome of interest. Begin answering: what is the development challenge you are addressing, what is your program’s theory, and what is your unit of randomization?

Week 9 Assignment

Week 10

Oct 26 & 28

Real-world challenges and alternatives to randomized evaluations.

Readings

  • Harlan and Appel, Failing in the Field, Ch. 2
  • Loiacono, Vargas and Tumusiime, “We Need More Respectful and Inclusive Experiments in Development Economics: A Proposal”
  • Ozler and Cuevas 2021, “Children on the move: the tale of a surprising spillover in humanitarian cash transfers to refugees” (blog)
  • Gupta and Kopper, “Power Calculations”
  • Discussion of “Pipe Dreams: Enforcing Payment for Water and Sanitation Services in Kenya” (interview)

Discussion

The distribution of individual outcomes on your policy of interest, and what it implies for program design — minimizing heterogeneous implementation, spillovers, non-compliance, and adverse effects.

Week 10 Assignment

Part II — Beyond Policy: The Broader Context

Week 11

Nov 2 & 4

Political order, long-term development, and economic growth.

Readings

  • Deborah Brautigam, “Building Leviathan: Revenue, State Capacity and Governance” (IDS Bulletin 33: 3)
  • Clements et al. 2026, “Aid Cuts Are Not Leading to Reforms in Sub-Saharan Africa” (CGD blog)
  • Berman and Lowery, “The 12 key results from the DOJ’s scathing Ferguson Report” (Washington Post)
  • Morten Jerven, “Briefing: For richer, for poorer: GDP revisions and Africa’s statistical tragedy” (African Affairs 112, no. 446, January 2013: 138–147)
  • Sampreet Goraya 2024, “The macroeconomic costs of the caste system in India”

Discussion

Measuring GDP, GDP per capita, and growth; displaying your country’s growth trajectory at 5-, 10-, 20-, and 40-year horizons.

Week 11 Assignment

Week 12

Nov 9 & 11

The deep origins of growth — geography, order, and colonial legacies. The role of weather, tropical agriculture, and technology. How colonial legacies shape trade patterns and governance today.

Readings

  • Paul Collier, The Bottom Billion, Chs. 3–4 (available online through Penn Library)
  • Michalopoulos and Papaioannou 2015, “The Long-Run Effects of the ‘Scramble for Africa’” (blog)
  • Leonard Wantchekon, “Breaking the Cycle of Rural Poverty: One Investment at a Time” (blog)

Discussion

Measures of geography, ecology, and growth, and how they correlate with growth rates in your place.

Week 12 Assignment

Week 13

Nov 16 & 18

Development as manipulable, an international policy tool, and an industry. The international donor community. The Trump revolution in international development.

Readings

  • Paul Collier, The Bottom Billion, Ch. 7 (available online through Penn Library)
  • Severine Autesserre, “Trouble in Peaceland” (Foreign Policy, Oct 6, 2015)
  • CGD, “How African Governments Responded to the 2025 Aid Shock” (Feb 10, 2026)
  • Iqbal Dhaliwal 2025, “Reflecting on the end of USAID” (JPAL blog)
  • NY Times 2025, “The DOGE Alum Asking if Foreign Aid is America’s Problem”

Discussion

The amount and type of donor programming in your place (see OECD stats), and what it is spent on.

Week 13 Assignment

Week 14

Nov 23 (no class Nov 25 — Thanksgiving Break; no assignment this week)

Big uncertainties: climate change.

Readings

  • Adom 2024, “The socioeconomic impact of climate change in developing countries over the next decades: A literature survey” (Heliyon 10(15): e35134)
  • Beynon and Wickstead, “Climate Change and Development in Three Charts: An Update” (CGD blog)
  • Wibbels et al. 2025, “Climate, Climate Adaptations, and Migration: Evidence from the U.S. Southwest Border” (report to Penn’s Kleinman Center)

Week 15

Nov 30 & Dec 2

Course conclusion. The promise and perils of field experiments. The frontier of what we know and what we can learn.

Readings

  • Dean Karlan and Jacob Appel, More than Good Intentions, Ch. 2
  • Iqbal Dhaliwal 2025, “The Evidence Effect: Ideas for the future of development” (JPAL blog)
