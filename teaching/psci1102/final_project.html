Final Assignment Guidelines

Due: Friday, December 11 at midnight
Length: approximately 12 pages, single-spaced
Late policy: Late papers will be deducted one full letter grade per day they are late. Please don’t turn in late papers.

Overview

Your final assignment is to design a good governance or poverty alleviation program in your country. You will justify your program design and provide a research design for evaluating whether or not the program/intervention works. Your program might be aimed at improving access to credit among poor households, improving childhood health, increasing teacher attendance, fostering the growth of new micro enterprises, improving the accountability of local governments, reducing corruption among tax collectors, reducing gender-based violence, etc. You should really tailor your program to: a) needs in the place that you have been studying this semester; and b) your policy interests. These final project reports should be approximately 12 pages single-spaced. Start now—it will not be possible to squeeze the requisite work in at the end of the semester and do well.

To be more precise, your job is to design a randomized (or impact) evaluation. A randomized evaluation involves assessing the impact of a program or intervention by randomly assigning it to recipients (the recipients might be households, teachers, tax collectors, small businesses, etc. depending on the nature of the program you propose) and comparing its effect to that of an untreated control group that does not receive the program/intervention. A randomized evaluation includes several ingredients, which you should build into your project report. Please organize your final project around each of these sections:

Required Sections

  1. Needs Assessment
    1. What is the need or shortcoming that your program is designed to address? Why is it important?
    2. How will you measure this need (i.e. what will be the outcome of interest for your program)? What is its incidence in the population of interest? Provide evidence from your country/place that justifies your claim that there is an important need.
  2. Program Theory
    1. What is the causal logic linking your intervention/program to the outcome of interest? How do you expect your intervention to impact the outcome of interest? Your program must have a logic whereby the intervention is expected to impact the need. That logic should be reflected in one or several hypotheses linking the intervention to the outcome.
    2. Describe your intervention. How does it work, what does it do? Importantly, how does it address the need indicated in (1) above?
    3. Be as explicit as possible in formalizing the logic whereby you expect your intervention to impact the outcome. Another way to think about this: What is the precise mechanism/s through which you expect your proposed intervention to work?
    4. Review research from related interventions in other places and link your program theory to the relevant literature.
  3. Program Design

    Describe how the program/intervention will be designed and implemented such that you’ll be able evaluate/test it. Doing so requires:

    1. A sample frame: Explain which individuals (or teachers or bureaucrats or small businesses or local governments or….) are candidates to receive the intervention or program. Assuming you do not reach every individual (or teacher or….) with your intervention, how will you choose the sample? This might involve thinking about issues like the population below some poverty rate, the part of the country/place where the programming will take place, whether the relevant unit is specific bureaucrats, whole government agencies, local governments, etc.
    2. A randomization procedure. Since this is to be a randomized evaluation, you need to explain how you will randomly assign the program to treatment and control groups.
    3. Power calculations: Power refers to your capacity to detect program effects of different sizes. Power depends on some things you can control (the size of the experiment, for instance) and some you cannot (i.e. how large an effect your intervention has). You will want to design your program to ensure you can detect a reasonably decent sized effect. What sized impact will you be able to detect given your program design? Note: I will send a separate doc on power calculations.

    In designing your program, directly address the following typical challenges:

    1. Spillovers/contamination: How can you design the program to be sure that those people (or teachers or tax collectors or parents or whatever) who receive the intervention/program do not contaminate the control group that does not? Is it possible, for instance, that treatment and control families/villages/businesses/bureaucrats will talk to each other and contaminate the design? If so, revisit the design in an effort to minimize the threat to your study.
    2. Non-compliance: Non-compliance occurs when those units assigned to treatment are not treated (for example, teachers might refuse to take part in an experimental training) or those assigned to the control group do receive treatment. What is the risk of non-compliance, and how can you minimize it?
    3. Adverse effects: Consider the possibility that your experiment might have negative consequences on the population you are aiming to help. Is it possible, for instance, that introducing a water system in one village might produce conflict with a nearby village that has not received the water system? Could a strengthening of the technical capabilities of the tax office lead to more sophisticated corruption by the tax authorities? If so, revisit the design in order to minimize the potential for adverse effects.
  4. Monitoring and Evaluation Plan

    In light of the Program Design:

    1. Develop a strategy for monitoring the program to make sure that it is being delivered as designed. Think to yourself: How might the implementers mess up the program, and how can you monitor it to ensure that doesn’t happen?
    2. Describe what data you will collect at baseline (i.e. before the program is introduced) and endline (i.e. when the program is complete) in order to assess whether or not the program has worked. What will you measure and how will you measure success?
  5. Cost-effectiveness Analysis

    Give a sense of how much the program will cost and how much the evaluation will cost. Given your program theory and the design of the program, evaluate the costs of the program and evaluation in light of what you expect its impact to be.

  6. External Validity Analysis

    Finally, give a sense of whether you think this project might work elsewhere. Above and beyond your specific place, what are the set of conditions and circumstances under which you think the program/intervention might apply.

  7. Statement on How/If You Used AI

    This should be as detailed as possible. See guidelines on AI use below.

We will cover all of this in class and readings, but you can find a series of brief, helpful guides here: J-PAL, Introduction to Randomized Evaluations.

Additional Guidelines

  • You should organize your paper around the points above.
  • You will need to go well beyond course materials to successfully complete this assignment. Any history, statistics, ideas, or language not clearly your own have to be cited. If they are not cited, that qualifies as plagiarism. Plagiarism is bad. Citations, endnotes, and the bibliography should be done in accord with the style indicated in the APA Style Manual or the Penn library site.
  • The project should be well written. This deserves your special attention. Even the best of projects is unacceptable if poorly written. Seek precision in your use of language. Complex language, big words, and sophisticated sentences (i.e. Sen-ian language) are not beneficial. In short, strive for clarity at every turn. I will grade grammar and organization. If you would like help with your writing, please refer to the Penn Writing Center.
  • AI use: The design of your impact evaluation should come from your own efforts, not AI. You can use AI to help with background research, but do not rely on it to generate either the intervention or the evaluation strategy. The first draft of your final project should be written by you without any AI support—You need to submit this version as an Appendix to your final project. After writing the first draft yourself you can subsequently use AI to improve the writing.

If you are unsure about any of these issues, please come talk to me during office hours or by appointment. Your final report will be much better if any confusion is cleared up before you start researching, never-mind writing.
