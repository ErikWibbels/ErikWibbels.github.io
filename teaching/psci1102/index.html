Why are some countries rich and some poor? Why are some households rich and some poor? This course introduces the analytical tools for understanding why development varies across the world and the practical tools for designing and evaluating policies aimed at alleviating poverty.

About the course

The course is organized into two parts. The first half focuses on the micro picture: household-level dynamics of poverty and development, including access to food and credit, the role of health and education, migration from village to city life, and day-to-day governance. You'll learn the practicalities of designing and evaluating governance and poverty-relief interventions, with a focus on field experiments. The second half turns to the macro picture: the political and economic foundations of sustained growth, including historical legacies, technological innovation, and political institutions.

Roughly 20 minutes of each Wednesday class is set aside for hands-on exercises: you'll choose a country to work on for the semester and use household survey or census microdata to build your own poverty measures, examine growth ingredients, trace family social mobility, and more — developing both analytical and practical data skills along the way.

This course is the first in a two-course sequence; the second is PSCI 3200, Global Development: Intermediate Topics in Politics, Policy, and Data, taught in the spring. Both are required for the minor in International Development.
