Week 9 Assignment

Turn in your assignment by Tuesday, 10/20 at 5p.

Your assignment this week is to gather background information on an intervention that you might be interested in pursuing for your final paper. It is not important that this week’s assignment turn into your final paper—the goal is simply to get some experience thinking carefully about a potential intervention and seeing what kind of research has previously been done on it.

First, review J-PAL’s introductory materials on impact evaluations here. Given what you know about your country’s challenges and your own interests, identify an area where you might intervene to test a policy intervention. You should feel free to think broadly—your intervention might be about reducing bribe-taking by police, increasing teacher attendance at school, improving labor market matching in the informal sector, promoting entrepreneurial activity, promoting productive migration within your country, promoting college attendance among high school graduates, reducing the incidence of gender-based violence, etc.

Your Intervention

Write a paragraph on each of these:

  • What is the development challenge you are trying to address?
  • What is your intervention to improve outcomes? Be as clear as you can: What is the unit of treatment (and thus randomization) and why? In other words, be clear about who exactly is going to be the recipient of your intervention. For instance, if your goal was to improve middle school math education, your treatment could go to students, students+their parents, teachers, principals, schools, etc. But it can’t be all of them because then we’d never know how exactly the intervention worked (or not). What justifies targeting your particular population?
  • What is the theory driving your intervention?

Existing Research

Explore what other research has been done in this area.

  • Use Eva Vivalt’s Build Your Own Meta-Analysis tool to explore the range of evaluation outcomes on your intervention of interest. Explain what you find. If Vivalt’s site does not have a meta-analysis for your intervention, look for a meta-analysis elsewhere and rely on it. If you can’t find one, explain what you find on policy outcomes that are tangentially related to yours. Note: it’s entirely fine if there isn’t already a large evidentiary basis around your intervention.
  • Use the World Bank’s Development Impact blog, the Poverty Action Lab (J-PAL) blog, and 3ie’s Evidence Matters to explore rigorous evaluations that might be of relevance to your intervention. Summarize what you find.
