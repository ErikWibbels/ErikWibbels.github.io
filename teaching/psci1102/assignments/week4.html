Week 4 Assignment

An Oral History of Intergenerational Mobility

Due: 5p on Tuesday, Sept 22 — complete the tasks below, write up your findings, and submit the document on Canvas.
  1. Read Chapter 2 in Krishna, particularly pages 35–45.
  2. Gather an oral history of intergenerational mobility from at least two branches of your family (if this isn’t possible, do it for a friend’s family). When doing so, explicitly pay attention to Steps 2–6 in the Krishna methodology and adjust as necessary — in other words, it might be inappropriate to be asking about entry and exit from poverty, but you can aim to get details and go back at least two generations on education, profession, income and assets.

    Note: If you’d rather gather information on four generations from a single branch of the family, feel free to do so.

    1. If you are uncomfortable with putting dollar figures onto paper, you should feel free to rescale (you could divide all numbers by 1,000, or multiply them by 10,000 for all I care! You don’t need to report your rescaling). But be sure that you rescale everything, including the historical values, to make them comparable to each other. The key to the exercise is to understand the triggers/mechanisms for upward and/or downward mobility through your family history.
    2. Please remember that inflation erodes the value of money. So if you have a great grandmother who bequeathed $10,000 to her children in 1948, that amounts to about $100,000 in today’s dollars. Be sensitive to these changes as you write up your family’s oral history. There are several historical inflation calculators available online that will give you the current value of past money values.

      For instance: measuringworth.com/calculators/uscompare/

  3. Your discussion of your family history should:
    1. Discuss the key triggers of upward and/or downward mobility. If your family’s story is one of stability, you should discuss the mechanisms that have promoted stability. As you do the assignment and write up your discussion, pay attention to:
      1. Were there any points where public policy seemed to make a big difference (for ex. the GI Bill or a scholarship or unemployment insurance or inheritance taxes or immigration programs/laws…)?
      2. What were the relative importance of entrepreneurial behavior (moving to a new place/across an ocean, starting a business, etc.) vs. education vs. networks (familial, business, etc.) vs. luck in the family trajectory?
    2. Discuss what you have learned about measuring the intergenerational transmission of income, assets, skills, etc. What were the hard and easy parts of the story to learn?
  4. Let me know in your written work whether or not you are willing to talk about your assignment during the classroom discussion. If you are, I might ask you to share examples as part of discussion points.
